7aIntro lead · maker’s voice — first person, specific, a little cranky
Breathing Index 🫁
Built because “Moderate” air kept wrecking my walks.
The official index averages every pollutant into one number, tuned to an average person. Nobody’s lungs are average — wildfire smoke at a “Moderate” 70 floored me in Connecticut, while Amsterdam air rated insufficient barely registered. So this keeps a diary instead. Rate a breath 1–4 when you think of it; the air gets saved alongside. After a while it knows what actually gets you — and warns you the way a friend would: “today looks like that smoky Tuesday that benched you.” Nothing leaves your phone. No account, no cloud.
7bIntro lead · quiet — no headline at all, reads like a letter
Breathing Index 🫁 a personal air diary
This app does one thing: it works out which air actually bothers your lungs, and tells you before you head out. You rate your breathing, 1 to 4, whenever you think of it. Everything measurable about that moment’s air is saved with the rating. That’s the whole trick — enough taps and it can say “smoke past 38 limits you, ozone alone never has.” The official indices never appear here. They had their chance. Nothing leaves your phone. No account, no cloud.
7cIntro lead · the user’s own question
Breathing Index 🫁
Why does “Moderate” air wreck your walk some days and not others?
Because the number is an average of averages. It can’t know that smoke gets you and ozone doesn’t — or the other way round. Your lungs know; they just don’t publish. This app takes their side of the story. Rate your breathing when you think of it, 1 to 4, and it learns your triggers from what you tap — then starts telling you what tomorrow will feel like. Nothing leaves your phone. No account, no cloud.
